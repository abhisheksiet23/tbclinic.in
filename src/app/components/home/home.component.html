Setting The Record Straight

Separating Facts From Fear

Misinformation about TB delays treatment and costs lives. Here is the truth.

Myth "TB is not curable."
Fact

With timely diagnosis and proper treatment, TB is treatable and curable in the vast majority of cases.

Myth "Only people from poor backgrounds get TB."
Fact

Tuberculosis can affect anyone — regardless of social or economic background.

Myth "Treatment can stop once symptoms improve."
Fact

Treatment should only be stopped under medical guidance. Stopping early can cause drug resistance.

Myth "Testing should wait until symptoms become severe."
Fact

Early diagnosis leads to better outcomes and reduces the spread to others.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About TB

Answers to the most common questions about tuberculosis, treatment, and our clinics.

Is tuberculosis (TB) curable?

Yes. Tuberculosis is fully curable with the right treatment. Most patients achieve complete recovery within 6 to 9 months when they adhere to their prescribed medication. Early diagnosis and consistent treatment adherence significantly improve outcomes.

How long does TB treatment take?

Standard first-line TB treatment lasts 6 months. Drug-resistant forms of TB may require 9 to 18 months of treatment. The exact duration depends on the type and severity of TB. Our expert team creates personalised treatment plans and monitors each patient closely.

Is TB contagious? How does it spread?

Pulmonary (lung) TB can spread through airborne droplets when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or speaks. Non-pulmonary forms — such as bone TB, lymph node TB, brain TB, and abdominal TB — are generally not contagious. With proper treatment, patients typically become non-infectious within a few weeks.

What are the early symptoms of TB?

Common early signs of TB include a persistent cough lasting more than two weeks, unexplained weight loss, night sweats, mild persistent fever, fatigue, loss of appetite, and occasionally coughing up blood. If you experience any of these symptoms, consult a TB expert promptly.

Do you treat all forms of TB, not just lung TB?

Yes. MEDCROSS treats all 8 recognised forms of tuberculosis: Pulmonary TB (lungs), Lymph Node TB, Bone & Spine TB, Brain TB (TB Meningitis), Abdominal TB, Genito-Urinary TB, Pleural TB (fluid around lungs), and Skin TB.
